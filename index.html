The model (math, for auditing)

1. Buckets and portfolio moments

Each bucket i has a one-year simple return Ri with E[Ri] = μi and SD(Ri) = σi (the editable assumptions). Weights w are reset to target once a year (annual rebalancing), so each year the portfolio return is Rp = Σi wiRi with

μp = wᵀμ        σp² = wᵀΣw,   Σij = ρij σi σj

Worked check, 50% low / 50% medium: σp = √(0.5²·0.15² + 0.5²·0.24² + 2·0.5·0.5·0.85·0.15·0.24) = √0.035325 = 18.79%. Set the sliders to 0/50/50/0 and the site shows the same.

2. Lognormal fit (moment matching)

1 + Rp is modeled as lognormal, LN(m, s²), with parameters chosen to match the first two moments exactly:

s² = ln(1 + σp² / (1+μp)²)       m = ln(1+μp) − s²/2

A weighted sum of lognormals is not exactly lognormal; this is the standard Fenton-Wilkinson style approximation. It keeps value above zero and gives the right-skew that makes mean > median.

3. Projection over T years

Years are i.i.d., so ln(VT/V0) ~ N(mT, s²T):

Percentile q:   V0 · exp(mT + zq s√T),   zq = Φ⁻¹(q)
Median:         V0 · emT
Mean (EV):      V0 · (1+μp)T
P(VT < V0):   Φ(−m√T / s)

The fan chart bands are the 10-90 and 25-75 prediction intervals; the table adds 2.5 and 97.5 (a 95% prediction interval). These are intervals for outcomes given the parameters. They do not include uncertainty about the parameters themselves (see the confidence intervals in the historical table), so the real spread is wider.

4. VaR, expected shortfall, and friends (1 year)

VaRα  = V0 (1 − em + s zα),            z0.05 = −1.645, z0.01 = −2.326
ESα   = V0 (1 − em + s²/2 Φ(zα − s) / α)
Normal VaRα = V0 (−μp − zασp)
Sharpe = (μp − rf) / σp,  rf = HYSA μ
Skew(LN) = (es²+2)√(es²−1)     ExKurt(LN) = e4s² + 2e3s² + 3e2s² − 6

ES follows from E[X·1{X<q}] = em+s²/2Φ((ln q − m − s²)/s) for X ~ LN(m, s²), with ln q = m + s zα. I checked the closed form against 200,000 simulated draws (Sam's plan: VaR95 14.2% vs 14.1%, ES95 18.6% vs 18.7%). The normal VaR is shown because it's what a lot of desk models quote; it ignores skew and overstates loss for very volatile buckets (it can even exceed 100%).

5. Max drawdown (Monte Carlo)

2,000 paths, monthly log steps ~ N(m/12, s²/12) (continuous rebalancing, same annual distribution), xorshift RNG with fixed seed 12345 and Box-Muller normals. MDD = maxt(1 − Vt/maxu≤tVu). The site reports the median and 95th percentile across paths and P(MDD ≥ 20%).

7. What the model leaves out

  • Fat tails beyond lognormal. Real crashes (1929, 2008) come more often than a lognormal says, so VaR/ES here are optimistic in the tails.
  • Parameter uncertainty and regime changes. μ is the hardest number in finance to estimate.
  • Correlations that jump toward 1 in a crisis; serial correlation (momentum, mean reversion).
  • Taxes, fees, inflation (all numbers are nominal), and the daily-reset decay of leveraged ETFs.
  • HYSA rates change with the Fed; 0.5% volatility is a stand-in for rate drift, not for loss risk (FDIC-insured).

Assumptions

Edit these and everything above updates. Returns are nominal (before inflation and taxes), yearly. Buckets are assumed rebalanced yearly. Outcomes use a lognormal model fit to each mix's mean and volatility. Correlations default to low/medium 0.85, low/extreme 0.6, medium/extreme 0.8, cash 0, and can be edited in the correlation matrix under Risk analytics.

Why these numbers

  • HYSA 4.0%, ~0% risk: top online savings rates are 4.00-4.10% APY right now (Bankrate, updated Sept 20, 2026). Rates are variable and will drift down if the Fed cuts. FDIC insured up to $250k.
  • Low risk 8% / 15%: the S&P 500 returned about 10.0% a year compounded from 1928-2025 (11.9% average year) with ~19% yearly swings, worst year -43.8% (Damodaran, NYU). This bucket mixes the index with lower-volatility staples (betas 0.2-0.6), and US stocks start from a high valuation (S&P P/E ~27), so I shaded both return and volatility down.
  • Medium risk 10% / 24%: QQQ compounded ~10.9% a year since 1999 but fell -81% from 2000 to 2002 and -33% in 2022. Nasdaq-100 trailing P/E is ~31-33 and market concentration is near dot-com levels, so I didn't assume the 2010s boom repeats.
  • Extreme risk 12% / 55%: no clean history for "moonshots". US bottom-decile small caps averaged 17.8% a year but compounded only 12.0% with ~38% swings. Individual speculative names and 3x funds swing far more (betas 2-6). 55% volatility means the typical path goes nowhere while a few paths explode.

Emerging tech themes

  • Memory "supercycle". AI servers need huge amounts of high-bandwidth memory. The growth screen shows it: Micron revenue +346% yoy, SK hynix +257%, Western Digital +44%. Memory is historically the most boom-bust corner of chips, so a peak here can reverse hard (MU trades 24% below its 52-week high already).
  • AI data-center plumbing. Networking and connectivity (Broadcom, Astera Labs, Amphenol +55% revenue, Celestica +62%). Less crowded than Nvidia itself, but priced richly (ALAB at 146x trailing earnings).
  • Quantum computing. IonQ just announced its 256-qubit system will power an Nvidia quantum research center (IonQ press release, Sept 23). Revenue is growing fast from a small base and the companies lose money. Pure extreme-bucket stuff.
  • Space. AST SpaceMobile, Rocket Lab. Launch failures and funding needs make these binary.
  • Robotics. Amplify launched concentrated "Top 10" ETFs on quantum (XQBT), space (XWNG), robotics (ROBX) and Asia memory (AHBM) this month, a sign of how much money is chasing these themes. New thematic ETFs often launch near peaks of enthusiasm.

Tech right now (Sept 2026), the honest read

  • Prices are near records again. The Nasdaq-100 hit its first record since June this week, after a September sell-off. Half of one recent 5-day run came from just five stocks (Financial Post, Sept 23).
  • Valuations: expensive vs history, cheaper vs last year. The S&P tech sector's forward P/E fell from ~32 last October to ~21, because earnings grew faster than prices; tech earnings estimates rose ~20% in three months (Truist via Yahoo Finance, Sept 21). Nasdaq trailing P/E is still ~31.
  • Volatility is high. UBS says US tech volatility is back to dot-com-bubble levels (semis ~69% annualized). iShares notes chips moved ±5% on a third of trading days in June-July.
  • Bubble debate is live. Bears point to concentration and AI capex (UBS says hyperscalers are on track for over 30% of all US capex in 2026). Bulls point to real earnings growth. Reuters (Sept 17) argues the bulls have more data; the Motley Fool notes a dot-com-style pop could keep QQQ under water until ~2042.
  • So: tech is a fair medium-risk bet for a long horizon, not a safe one. Buying in pieces over a few months cuts the regret if it drops right after you buy.
